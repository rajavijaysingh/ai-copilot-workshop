## SECTION 3 — SYSTEMS CHECK: ETHICS / AI Ethics Court (Slides 23–29)

### Slide 23 — Check Card
- ⚖️ **SYSTEMS CHECK — ETHICS** · *The machine is never guilty. The pilot always is.*
- Strip: INSTRUMENTS + COMMS lit, SYSTEMS pulsing.

### Slide 23b — "Why Ethics?" (facilitator teaches, 90 seconds)
- Screen shows a simple visual: a **balance scale** — one side has a gavel, the other has a laptop.
- Below: **POWER WITHOUT RULES = DANGER**
- Under that, 4 lines:
  - 🤝 **INTEGRITY** — Be honest about what's yours and what's AI's
  - 🔍 **DILIGENCE** — Check the work. Verify. Don't trust blindly.
  - 🛡️ **DUTY TO OTHERS** — Don't use AI to harm, trick, or expose others
  - 📣 **TRANSPARENCY** — If AI helped, say so. No hiding.

**Facilitator notes (90 sec):**

"Before we open our court, let me ask you something. Every serious profession in the world has an ethics code — rules about what you WON'T do, even when you CAN.

Doctors CAN prescribe any drug — but their code says: first, do no harm. They won't give you medicine you don't need just because they could.

Lawyers CAN know a client's darkest secrets — but their code says: protect that trust. They won't share it, even if it benefits them.

Engineers CAN cut corners on a building to save money — but their code says: public safety first. They won't risk lives for a shortcut.

Financial professionals CAN trade on insider information — but their code says: be fair, be transparent, earn trust honestly.

Every one of these codes comes down to the same idea: just because you CAN do something doesn't mean you SHOULD. Power without rules is dangerous. And the people who last in any career — the ones people trust, promote, and respect — are the ones who chose ethics over shortcuts.

Now — AI has just handed YOU more power than any generation of students has ever had. You can generate essays, fake images, copy someone's style, expose private information — in seconds. So the question is: what's YOUR code? What will you refuse to do, even when it's easy?

That's what we're about to find out. Three real people used AI. Three complaints have been filed. You decide: guilty or not guilty."

### Slide 24 — Court Setup
- Screen: **AI ETHICS COURT** · *3 cases. 3 defendants. You're the justice system.*
- Below: a courtroom layout showing PROSECUTION (left), DEFENSE (right), JURY (center). Each labeled with squad numbers for Case 1.
- Sub-line: *The AI is not on trial. The HUMAN who used it is.*

**Facilitator notes — setting up the court (30 sec):**

"Here's how it works. I'm going to read you three real-world cases — adults who used AI and got into legal trouble. For each case, I'll assign squads. Some of you argue the person is GUILTY. Some argue NOT GUILTY. The rest are the JURY — you vote.

Remember: the AI didn't choose anything. It did what it was told. The question is always — did the HUMAN cross the line?"

Announce Case 1 rotation: "Squads 1 and 2 — you're prosecution. Squads 3 and 4 — defence. Everyone else — you're the jury."

**Squad rotation:**

| Case | Prosecution | Defense | Jury |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squads 1 + 2 | Squads 3 + 4 | Squads 5–12 |
| 2 | Squads 5 + 6 | Squads 7 + 8 | Squads 1–4, 9–12 |
| 3 | Squads 9 + 10 | Squads 11 + 12 | Squads 1–8 |

Every squad argues once. Every squad is jury twice.

**Running each case (3 min per case):**
1. Show the FIR slide. Read it aloud dramatically (like a police report).
1b. Give arguing squads a 'starter argument' if they freeze — 2 prompt lines per side are pre-printed on their Co-Pilot Code card. They don't have to use them, but it removes the blank-mind panic for non-native English speakers.
2. Prosecution squads huddle 30 sec → ONE speaker argues 45 sec.
3. Defense squads huddle 30 sec → ONE speaker argues 45 sec.
4. Jury (8 squads): hold up GUILTY / NOT-GUILTY cards. Count majority.
5. Facilitator drops the verdict + the rule (30 sec).

### Slide 25 — CASE 1: "THE CODE LEAK"

```
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
⚖️ CASE NO. 001
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

COMPLAINANT: TechNova Solutions Pvt Ltd, Bengaluru
DEFENDANT:   Rahul Mehta, Software Developer

CHARGE:      Leaking confidential client data

WHAT HAPPENED:
Rahul was debugging code at midnight before a
deadline. He pasted proprietary company code into
ChatGPT to find the bug faster. The code contained
a client's database login credentials. The company's
security team flagged the breach. The client is now
threatening legal action.

RAHUL SAYS:  "I was just trying to fix a bug. I
didn't even notice the credentials were in there.
Everyone uses AI to code faster."
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

- Corner: **GUILTY? or NOT GUILTY?**

**Facilitator notes — talking points each side might raise:**

| Prosecution (GUILTY) | Defense (NOT GUILTY) |
|---|---|
| He signed an NDA — he's responsible for what leaves the system, period. | He didn't intend to leak anything — it was an honest mistake under pressure. |
| "I didn't notice" isn't a legal defense — negligence IS a breach. | The company should have tools to prevent sensitive data from being pasted. It's a systems failure, not a personal one. |
| The client's data is now in a third-party AI system FOREVER — the harm is real and irreversible. | Every developer uses AI to debug. The company benefits from faster output — they should have trained staff on what's safe to share. |
| If a doctor accidentally gave the wrong medicine, "I didn't notice" wouldn't save them. Same standard. | He was working at midnight to MEET THEIR DEADLINE. The pressure came from the company itself. |

**How to evaluate the jury vote:**
- This case tests: **Does intent matter when real harm occurs?** The mature answer: intent reduces moral blame but doesn't erase legal responsibility. You're still accountable for what you feed AI, even accidentally.
- Reasonable verdict: **GUILTY of negligence** (not malice). He should have checked. The company ALSO failed (no guardrails), but that doesn't clear him.

**Rule revealed:** **PROTECT** — be careful what data you feed AI. Once it's in, you can't take it back. Always check before you paste.

### Slide 26 — CASE 2: "THE VOICE CLONE"

```
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
⚖️ CASE NO. 002
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

COMPLAINANT: Family of late singer S.P. Balasubrahmanyam
DEFENDANT:   Deepak Sharma, YouTube Creator

CHARGE:      Voice cloning & impersonation for profit

WHAT HAPPENED:
Deepak used an AI tool to clone the voice of
legendary singer S.P. Balasubrahmanyam — who
passed away in 2020 — and released 5 "new songs"
on YouTube. He earned ₹2 lakh in ad revenue. He
never disclosed the voice was AI-generated. Fans
believed they were unreleased tracks and shared
them emotionally.

DEEPAK SAYS: "I'm honouring his legacy. The songs
are my original compositions — only the voice is
AI. I'm keeping his music alive for new fans."
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

- Corner: **GUILTY? or NOT GUILTY?**

**Facilitator notes — talking points each side might raise:**

| Prosecution (GUILTY) | Defense (NOT GUILTY) |
|---|---|
| SPB is dead — he cannot consent. Using a dead person's voice without family permission is identity theft. | He's a fan paying tribute. Musicians cover songs all the time — how is this different? |
| Fans were DECEIVED — they cried, shared emotional messages, believing these were real unreleased tracks. That's manipulation. | He never explicitly claimed they were "real SPB songs." People assumed. That's their mistake. |
| He earned ₹2 lakh. This isn't tribute — it's profit from someone else's identity. | The compositions are 100% original. Only the voice timbre was cloned. The creative work is his. |
| The family never approved this. Their grief is being used as content. | SPB would have wanted his voice to live on. Art belongs to the public after an artist passes. |
| If someone deepfaked YOUR parent's voice to sell things, would that be okay? | Copyright law doesn't cover a "voice." There's no clear legal violation yet. |

**How to evaluate the jury vote:**
- This case tests: **Can you "honour" someone while profiting from their identity without consent?** The emotional weight is heavy — SPB is beloved and gone. The mature answer: tribute requires transparency and family consent. Profiting without disclosure crosses from homage to exploitation.
- Reasonable verdict: **GUILTY of impersonation for profit.** The non-disclosure is the killer — had he labeled every video "AI-generated tribute, not real SPB," this would be a different conversation.

**Rule revealed:** **CREDIT** — be transparent. If AI created it, say so. If it's someone else's voice/identity, you don't own it just because you pressed a button.

### Slide 27 — CASE 3: "THE ART HEIST"

```
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
⚖️ CASE NO. 003
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

COMPLAINANT: Neha Kulkarni, Freelance Illustrator
DEFENDANT:   BrightAds Pvt Ltd, Advertising Agency

CHARGE:      Copying an artist's style using AI

WHAT HAPPENED:
BrightAds needed illustrations for a ₹15 lakh
ad campaign. Instead of hiring an artist, they
used AI and typed: "Generate art in the style of
Neha Kulkarni's Instagram illustrations." The
output looked almost identical to Neha's work.
They won the client contract. Neha was never
contacted, credited, or paid.

BRIGHTADS SAYS: "You can't copyright a style.
AI created something new — it's not a copy. We
saved the client money. That's just business."
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

- Corner: **GUILTY? or NOT GUILTY?**

**Facilitator notes — talking points each side might raise:**

| Prosecution (GUILTY) | Defense (NOT GUILTY) |
|---|---|
| They NAMED her in the prompt. They deliberately targeted her specific style. That's not inspiration — that's theft with extra steps. | Copyright law protects specific artworks, not a "style." Every artist is influenced by others. That's how art works. |
| Neha lost a ₹15 lakh job to a machine trained on her own work. She was harmed financially and professionally. | BrightAds never used her actual images. The AI generated new, original pixels. Nothing was copied. |
| If you walk into a shop and say "make me a bag exactly like that designer's," that's a counterfeit. Same principle. | Innovation means disruption. Cheaper solutions replace expensive ones — that's capitalism, not crime. |
| They profited directly from her reputation (using her NAME in the prompt) without paying her. That's passing off. | Should we ban AI art entirely? Where do you draw the line? All artists learn from other artists. |
| Replace "AI" with "an intern" — "I told my intern to copy Neha's style." Still okay? | The client got what they needed at a lower cost. No consumer was deceived. The end product serves its purpose. |

**How to evaluate the jury vote:**
- This case tests: **Is it okay to replicate someone's livelihood using AI just because it's technically legal (for now)?** The mature answer: legal ≠ ethical. The prompt naming her specifically shows intent to exploit, not just "be inspired by."
- Reasonable verdict: **GUILTY of unfair practice.** The deliberate naming in the prompt converts "inspiration" into "targeting." Even if the law hasn't caught up, the ethics are clear.

**Rule revealed:** **THINK FIRST** — just because AI makes it easy doesn't make it right. Ask yourself: would I do this to their face?

---

**General evaluation guidance for the facilitator:**

When dropping the verdict after jury votes:
1. **Acknowledge the grey.** "This is a genuinely hard case. There's no perfect answer — but here's how professionals would see it..."
2. **Don't shame the "wrong" voters.** "If you voted differently — good. That means you were thinking. The point isn't to agree with me. The point is to THINK before you act."
3. **Connect to the students' future.** "When YOU use AI in your career — coding, creating, working — you'll face versions of this case. The code helps you decide in advance."

Rahul, Deepak, BrightAds — they all had a reasonable excuse. 'I was just trying to be faster.' 'I'm just a fan.' 'It's just business.' But in every case, someone was harmed because the human didn't stop to think.

These four rules are your code as a pilot. Not because someone will punish you — but because the people who build real careers are the ones others TRUST. Write them on your Co-Pilot Code card. Systems check — cleared."

---

**Edge case handling:**
- **A squad refuses to argue their assigned side:** Reframe: "You're a LAWYER. Lawyers defend guilty people every day. Your job isn't to agree — it's to find the best argument." If they still refuse, swap them to jury and pull another squad. Don't force it.
- **Dead silence from arguing squads:** Use the pre-printed starter arguments: "Let me give you a hint — look at your Co-Pilot Code card, prosecution side."
- **Scoring dispute:** Announce upfront: "All scoring is final. The facilitator's word is law." Keep scores close — if one squad runs away, others disengage.
