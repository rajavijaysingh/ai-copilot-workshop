## SECTION 2 — COMMS CHECK: COMMUNICATION / Prompt Battle (Slides 14–22)

### Slide 14 — Check Card

- Strip: INSTRUMENTS lit, COMMS pulsing.

### Slide 15 — "What's a Prompt?" (facilitator teaches, 90 seconds)
- Screen shows a simple visual: a stick figure → speech bubble → robot → output.
- One line below: **YOUR WORDS IN → AI'S WORK OUT**
- Below that, a box: **WHAT MAKES A GOOD PROMPT?**
  - 🎯 **WHO** — Who is the audience? (a 5-year-old? a teacher? a friend?)
  - 📐 **WHAT** — What format? (a list? a story? a rap? an email?)
  - 📏 **HOW MUCH** — How long? (3 lines? 1 paragraph? 30 seconds?)
  - 🎭 **WHAT TONE** — Fun? Serious? Sarcastic? Encouraging?
  - ⚡ **ONE EXTRA RULE** — Add one constraint that makes it interesting (e.g., "don't use the word 'good'")

**Facilitator notes (90 sec):**

"A prompt is just the instruction you give AI. Watch the difference:"

Show verbally (or point to screen):
- **Lazy prompt:** "Write about exams." → AI gives a boring paragraph nobody wants.
- **Pilot prompt:** "Write a 30-second pep talk for a nervous student before an exam. Make it sound like a coach in a locker room. Keep it under 5 sentences." → AI gives something you'd actually want to hear.

"Same AI. Same topic. The ONLY difference? Five extra seconds of thinking. That's what these 5 ingredients do — they turn a vague wish into a clear instruction. You're about to prove this."

*(Leave the 5 attributes visible on screen during the writing phase — it's their cheat-sheet.)*

### Slide 16 — Mission Briefing + Rules
- Screen: **PROMPT BATTLE — THE RULES**
- Three lines, big:
  - 1️⃣ TWO ROUNDS: one easy mission, one hard mission
  - 2️⃣ Write the BEST prompt you can for each (3 min per round)
  - 3️⃣ 1 random prompt goes LIVE + 1 gold standard — then you score the rest

**Facilitator notes for setup (2 min):**

1. Explain: "Round 1 is your warm-up — a simple mission. Round 2 is where real prompt skill shows. The 5 attributes on screen are your toolkit — use them."
2. All 12 squads collaborate — write ONE prompt per round on their **Prompt Bet Slip** (front = Round 1, back = Round 2).
3. Remind them: "The more ingredients from that list you use, the better your AI output will be. But don't just list them robotically — make it a clear instruction a human could follow too."

### Slide 17 — ROUND 1: EASY MISSION
- Screen top (stays small): The 5 prompt attributes as icons (WHO 🎯 WHAT 📐 HOW MUCH 📏 TONE 🎭 EXTRA ⚡)
- Center, big: **ROUND 1 — EASY MISSION:**
- **"Get AI to convince a teenager to drink more water."**
- Timer: **3:00**

**Why this mission works:**
- Universally relatable (everyone drinks water / doesn't drink enough).
- Many possible interpretations: funny ad? health facts? scare tactics? a meme caption? a rap? a text from a friend?
- Simple enough that even a lazy prompt will produce SOMETHING — but a great prompt will produce something clearly better.

### Slide 18 — ROUND 1: LIVE FIRE
- Screen: **LIVE FIRE — ROUND 1** · typing bay frame.

**Arena mechanic (same both rounds):**
1. Collect 12 slips (30 sec, captains pass forward).
2. **Random pull:** Shuffle face-down, pull 1 blindly. Announce squad name — instant drama.
3. **Type it EXACTLY as written** — no corrections, no edits, even if it has typos. Room watches output generate in real time.
4. Room scores: **1–5 fingers**. Helper notes average. That's the squad's score.
5. **Then type the Gold Standard:** "Now watch what happens when the prompt uses all 5 ingredients." Type the facilitator's pre-prepared prompt live. Room watches the output.
6. **Let the contrast land.** Pause 5 seconds. "Same AI. Same mission. Different pilot." No further lecture needed — the screen just proved it.

**Gold Standard prompt for Round 1 (type this second):**
> "Write a 4-line text message from a friend trying to guilt-trip a teenager into drinking water. Make it dramatic and funny, like they're dying of thirst on their behalf. Use gen-z slang. End with a water emoji."

### Slide 19 — ROUND 2: HARD MISSION
- Screen top: Same 5 prompt attributes (their cheat-sheet).
- Center, big: **ROUND 2 — HARD MISSION:**
- **"Get AI to help a student figure out what career might suit them — without asking the student any questions."**
- Timer: **3:00**

**Why this mission works:**
- Directly connects to the workshop's career-anxiety theme (plants the seed for the Flight Plan check).
- Genuinely hard — how do you prompt AI to give career advice without a back-and-forth conversation? Forces creative prompt engineering.
- Many interpretations: a quiz it generates? a personality-based framework? a "if you like X try Y" list? a story that surfaces interests?
- The quality gap between lazy and sharp prompts will be HUGE here.

**Gold Standard prompt for Round 2 (type this second):**
> "Create a '5 Clues About Me' career discovery game for a teenager. Give 5 categories: what I do for fun, what frustrates me, what I'd fix in the world, what I lose track of time doing, what people ask me for help with. For each category, give 3 example answers and show what career family they might point to. Keep it casual and fun — not like a school test. Format as a simple table."

### Slide 21 — Peer Scoring (both rounds)
- Screen: **YOUR TURN TO JUDGE** · *Read the prompt you received. Score 1–5. The gold standard is your benchmark.*
- Timer: 3:00

**Facilitator notes — peer scoring:**
1. Redistribute remaining 11 slips per round (pass one table to the left — ensuring no squad scores their own).
2. Each squad READS the received prompt aloud within their group.
3. Scores 1–5: How close is this to the gold standard? Did it use the 5 attributes?
4. Write score on slip, pass back. Helper collects.
5. Final squad score for Comms Check: Peer score average (1–5) × 4 = max 20 points. Live squads: room finger-vote average × 4. Same scale for everyone.

**BACKUP PLAN (if AI tool crashes or lags):**
Pre-run both gold standard prompts before the session and save outputs as screenshots on your laptop. If the live tool fails, show the screenshot immediately: "Here's what it produced when I tested this morning." No one knows the difference. Never let 134 kids watch a loading screen.
