## FACILITATOR THEME GUIDE — The Pilot Metaphor

### The Core Metaphor

| Concept | Pilot language | Avoid saying |
|---|---|---|
| Students | Pilots | "Guys," "kids," "class" |
| AI | Co-pilot / right-hand seat | "The tool," "the app," "the software" |
| The workshop | A flight / pre-flight check | "This session," "this workshop" |
| The workshop games | System checks (Instruments, Comms, Systems, Flight Plan) | "Activities," "tasks," "gates" |
| Using AI well | Steering / flying | "Using," "operating" |
| Using AI badly | Crashing / flying blind | "Misusing," "being irresponsible" |
| Their future career | Their runway / their destination | "Your job," "your career path" |
| Not knowing the future | Uncharted airspace | "Uncertainty," "not knowing" |
| The fear about AI | The turbulence | "Anxiety," "worry" |
| Graduating / leaving school | Takeoff / cleared for takeoff | "Graduating," "going to college" |
| The workshop ending | Cleared for takeoff | "We're done," "that's all" |

---

### Transition Phrases (use between games)

- "Buckle in — we're moving to the next check."
- "Check [X] — cleared. Let's keep climbing."
- "Steady altitude. Next check ahead."
- "We hit some turbulence there — but you handled it. Moving on."
- "The runway's getting closer. Two checks to go."

---

### Praise Language (use when squads do well)

- "That's pilot-level thinking."
- "You just out-flew the machine."
- "THAT is what steering looks like."
- "Smooth landing on that one."
- "Your squad just earned altitude."
- "Co-pilot couldn't have done that alone."

---

### Challenge Language (use to push energy up)

- "Scared pilots bet 1. Who's betting 3?"
- "That was autopilot mode — I need PILOT mode from you."
- "The co-pilot is waiting for YOUR instruction. Don't be vague."
- "Any squad flying lazy will feel it on the scoreboard."
- "You're cleared for this — fly with confidence."

---

### Key Moments Where the Metaphor MUST Land

| Moment | What to say | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Opening (Slide 3) | "You're not the passenger. You're the PILOT." | Sets the frame for everything |
| After Instruments Check | "Instruments reading clearly — you know where the machine breaks." | Pilots check instruments before relying on them |
| After Comms Check | "Comms online. Same mission, different steering — your communication makes the difference." | Communication = talking to ATC clearly |
| After Systems Check | "Systems armed. The machine has no override button — YOU are the override." | Ethics = the human override switch |
| After Flight Plan | "Flight plan filed. The co-pilot doesn't know where to go — only YOU do." | Navigation = career direction is human choice |
| Boarding Pass payoff | "That was the turbulence you walked in with. Look at your flight log — that's your runway." | Fear = turbulence; possibility = runway |
| Closing | "All checks complete. Cleared for takeoff. And YOU'RE the pilot." | Full circle back to the promise |

---

### The Metaphor's Hidden Job

The pilot/co-pilot metaphor isn't decoration — it does three psychological things:

1. **Gives students STATUS.** They're not "learners" being taught — they're "pilots" being certified. That's a power frame, not a student frame. Teenagers respond to status.

2. **Reframes AI from threat to tool.** A co-pilot is useful but subordinate. It never makes the decision. It never gets blamed. It never flies alone. This dissolves "AI will replace me" without arguing against it directly.

3. **Makes the workshop feel earned, not endured.** "Clearing checks" and "earning takeoff" is a progression narrative. They're not sitting through a session — they're ACHIEVING something. Each check lit = dopamine hit.

---

### What NOT to Do

- ❌ Don't overdo it. One pilot phrase per transition is enough. If EVERY sentence is aviation-themed it becomes a joke.
- ❌ Don't mix metaphors. No sports analogies, no ship analogies, no cooking analogies. Stick to aviation the whole way.
- ❌ Don't explain the metaphor. Never say "so in our pilot metaphor, AI is like a co-pilot because..." — just USE the language. They'll absorb it without instruction.
- ❌ Don't use it during the quiet Flight Plan moment. That's the one time to drop the game frame and be human. "Fill your Flight Log" is enough — then just be present, not performative.
