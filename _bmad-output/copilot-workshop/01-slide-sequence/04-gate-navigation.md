### Slide 40 — Design Your Future (DROP THE VOLUME)
- Visual shift: quieter, **dawn-gradient background** (signals tone change). One line: *Fill your Flight Log. Take your time.*
- NO timer ring — protect the quiet. This is the one slide that breathes.
- *(Facilitator walks softly. Optional: quiet background music. 5–7 minutes of solo writing.)*

**Facilitator notes — managing the quiet:**

- Don't talk for the first 2 minutes. Let silence work.
- After 2 min, softly walk and offer nudges to stuck students: "What do you do when nobody's making you do anything?" / "If you could fix one annoying thing in daily life, what would it be?"
- Don't rush. This is the moment the whole loud machine was built to protect.

### Slide 40b — Share + Captain Picks (4 min)
- Screen: **WHO WANTS TO SHARE?** · *Your boldest "AI + Me" career. Stand up. 20 seconds. Go.*

**Facilitator notes:**

1. "Anyone in the room — if you came up with something wild, stand up and tell us your AI + Me career in 20 seconds. What you enjoy + what you'd solve + AI = what?"
2. Take 4–5 volunteers from the room (don't force — hands only). Cheer each one. Keep it fast and celebratory.
3. After volunteers: "Captains — your squad also brainstormed earlier. If your squad has a banger that nobody's said yet, stand up and pitch it. 20 seconds."
4. Take 3–4 captain pitches (not all 12 — only if energy is there).
5. Room cheers. Facilitator picks top 3 for points (+3 / +2 / +1).

*(The point is celebration, not evaluation. Every answer is valid. Reward boldness, not correctness.)*

---

**Edge case handling:**
- **Dead silence during volunteer pitches:** Have 2 captains pre-warned: "I might call on you." Or seed it: "I overheard Squad 7's idea while walking around and it was great — want to share?"
- **Non-English speakers struggling with Flight Log:** Explicitly allow: "Write in ANY language. Draw pictures. Use a mix. The Flight Log is for YOU — nobody else needs to read it."
- **The quiet moment never materializes:** Beyond the dawn-gradient on screen — dim the room lights. The sensory change forces the mood shift faster than words.
