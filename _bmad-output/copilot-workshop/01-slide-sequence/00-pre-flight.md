## SECTION 0 — PRE-FLIGHT (Slides 1–4)

### Slide 1 — Title (hold before doors / on entry)
- **Center, huge:** ✈️ **AI IS YOUR CO-PILOT**
- Sub, small, amber: *You're the pilot. AI's in the right-hand seat.*
- Bottom: dim Pre-Flight Check Strip (all 4 checks grey).
- *3-sec test:* They read the title and the promise. Done.

**Facilitator notes — BEFORE students enter:**

1. **Room setup:** Tables arranged for 12 squads (10–12 per table). Each table has a pre-loaded **squad envelope** containing: voting cards, prompt bet slip, squad assignment card, Co-Pilot Code card. Boarding Passes and Flight Logs stacked at each table (students grab during squad formation).
2. **Screen:** Slide 1 already up and holding. Music playing if you have a speaker (upbeat, low volume — signals "something is about to happen").
3. **Your position:** Stand at the front, visible, smiling. Don't start talking until the room is mostly seated. Let the screen do the work while they settle.
4. **Helper brief (30 sec):** Your scoring helper knows: they track 12 squads, one score per check, max 20 each. Show them the scoreboard sheet. They sit to the side with a clear view of the room.

**IF STUDENTS ARE ALREADY IN THE ROOM (cold-open scenario):**

Skip the peaceful entry. They're seated, chatting, distracted. Your sequence:

1. **Grab attention with the signal — untrained.** Walk to front, put up Slide 1, then immediately:
   - "HEY! When I raise my hand and say PILOTS — you say READY. Let's try it. PILOTS —"
   - They'll be confused. Do it again, louder: "That was terrible. AGAIN. PILOTS —"
   - Third time it'll click. You now have silence and a tool. This takes 30 seconds.
2. **Jump to Slide 2 immediately.** Don't give the opening speech yet. "Open the envelope on your table. 3 minutes: squad name, captain, cheer. GO." Let them DO something before they LISTEN.
3. **Give the opening speech (Slide 3) AFTER squads form.** Once they have team identity and are invested, they'll actually hear you. Moving the speech by 3 minutes costs nothing and gains full attention.
4. **Boarding Pass:** Distribute during squad formation OR have them pre-placed on tables. "While your captain creates the cheer — everyone grab a Boarding Pass and fill it out quietly."

*(The rest of the session is identical from Slide 3 onward.)*

---

### Slide 2 — BOARDING (0:00–0:03, 3 min)
- One word, screen-filling: **BOARDING**
- Tiny corner prompt: *Squad name + captain + cheer → 3 min*
- Subtle airport split-flap board texture behind the word.

**Facilitator notes (3 min):**

"Welcome! You're at the right check. Quick — I need three things from every table before we take off:"

1. "Pick a squad name — anything you want."
2. "Pick a captain — they'll be your spokesperson today."
3. "Create a squad CHEER — 3 seconds max. A war cry, a clap pattern, a chant, whatever. Something we'll hear every time you score. You have 3 minutes. GO."

*(Walk the room while they do this. Energy should be noisy and fast. If some tables are slow, call out squads that are done: "Squad Rocket already has a cheer! Let's hear it!" — then cheer them. This gets competitive FAST.)*


**Quick test (30 sec):** After 3 min, go around the room rapid-fire: "Squad 1 — name and cheer! Squad 2 — name and cheer!" Each gets 5 seconds. Cheer the loudest ones. This takes ~1 min for 12 squads and locks in the squad identity.

**While they work:** Students should also grab a **Boarding Pass** from the stack on their table and fill it out:
- Name, squad name
- The private line: "When I think about my future job, I worry about: ______"
- "Fold it. Keep it in your pocket. You'll need it before landing."

*(Don't explain WHY. The mystery is intentional — it pays off at Slide 41.)*

---

### Slide 3 — The Lie / The Frame (0:03–0:07, ~4 min)
- Full-bleed split image: **left = lone pilot in storm. right = pilot + co-pilot, calm.**
- Single overlaid line, bottom: *Which cockpit do you want?*
- No bullet points. The facilitator's voice carries the script. The image IS the argument.

**Facilitator notes (the big opening speech, ~90 sec):**

*(Wait for silence. Use the "Pilots —" / "— ready!" signal for the first time here. Rehearse it: "When I say 'Pilots' and raise my hand, you say 'ready' and go quiet. Let's try it." Do it twice until it's crisp. This is your crowd-control tool for the rest of the session.)*

Then, with energy:

"Look at this image. Two cockpits. One pilot flying alone in a storm — overwhelmed, one mistake away from crashing. One pilot with a co-pilot — calm, supported, two sets of eyes on the instruments.

Here's the lie everyone's selling you: that AI is coming for your seat. That it's going to replace you. That by the time you graduate, there won't be jobs left.

Wrong.

AI just got handed to YOU — your generation. The most powerful tool any generation of students has EVER held. You're not the passenger. You're not being replaced. You're the PILOT. And AI? It's in the right-hand seat. It's your co-pilot.

But here's the thing about co-pilots: they're useless if the pilot doesn't know how to fly. So today, we're going to prove — in four games — that YOU know how to fly. Ready?"

*(Pause. Let it land. 2 seconds of silence. Then move to Slide 4.)*

---

### Slide 4 — The Four Checks (0:07–0:10, ~3 min)
- The Flight-Check Strip animates up to center, large: **INSTRUMENTS · COMMS · SYSTEMS · FLIGHT PLAN**
- Below, amber: *Clear all four checks. Earn your takeoff.*
- Then strip shrinks back to the bottom edge where it lives for the rest of the show.

**Facilitator notes (setup the game frame, ~2 min):**

"Every pilot has to clear a pre-flight check before takeoff. Four system checks:

- **INSTRUMENTS — Knowledge** — Can you read the machine? Do you know where it's reliable and where it breaks?
- **COMMS — Communication** — Can you talk to your co-pilot clearly? Can you steer AI with precise instructions?
- **SYSTEMS — Ethics** — Are your override switches working? Do you know when to say NO to the machine?
- **FLIGHT PLAN — Navigation** — Do you know where YOU'RE going? Has your destination been charted?

Each check is a game. Each game earns points. The squad with the most points at the end wins the **Co-Pilot Cup.**"

*(Show the scoreboard briefly — 12 squad names, four columns, all zeros. Then:)*

"Scoring rules — simple: each check is worth max 20 points. Four checks = max 80. I'll explain each game's scoring before we play it. For now — just know every game counts, every squad is alive until the end, and the final check is where champions are made.

One more thing: when I raise my hand and say 'Pilots' — you say 'ready' and go silent. Let's lock that in."

*(Practice the signal one more time. Then:)*

"Instruments check: Knowledge. Let's see if you can out-think the machine."

*(Move to Slide 5 — Instruments Check opens.)*
