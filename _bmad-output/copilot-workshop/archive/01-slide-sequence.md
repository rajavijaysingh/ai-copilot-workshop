# ✈️ AI IS YOUR CO-PILOT — Screen Slide Sequence

**Design law:** The screen is a jumbotron, not a worksheet. Every slide passes the **3-second rule** — one idea, readable from the back row. All reading-text lives on the handouts. Slides are spectacle, timer, scoreboard, and reveal only.

**Visual language (keep consistent all deck):**
- Background: deep night-sky navy (`#0B1437`).
- Accent / "lit" color: runway amber-gold (`#FFC23C`).
- Text: near-white (`#F5F7FF`). Single bold sans-serif (e.g. Montserrat / Poppins), ALL-CAPS for headlines.
- Recurring motif: the **Flight-Check Strip** — 4 gates (KNOWLEDGE · COMMUNICATION · ETHICS · NAVIGATION) across the bottom. Gates start dim grey; each lights amber when its game ends. This is the progress bar AND the story.
- One font weight change = one level of hierarchy. No more. White space is the co-pilot.

## SECTION 1 — GATE 1: KNOWLEDGE / AI MythBusters (Slides 5–13)

- Sub: *Can you out-think the machine?*

### Slide 5b — How to Bet (show once, stays in memory)
- Visual: a hand holding a TRUE/FALSE card + another hand showing 1, 2, or 3 fingers.

  - **HOLD UP: your bet (1, 2, or 3 fingers)**

- Sub: *Max bet = 3. Big risk, big reward. Scared pilots bet 1.*
- *(This is the only "rules" slide — keep it up for 15 sec, then move.)*

### Slides 6–12 — Myth Cards (7 rounds, one per slide)
- Each slide: ONE statement, centered, large. A **5-second countdown ring** in the corner. Nothing else.
  - 6: "AI IS ALWAYS FAIR AND UNBIASED."
  - 7: "AI IS BECOMING CONSCIOUS — IT CAN THINK AND FEEL."
  - 8: "ONCE AI IS SET UP, IT RUNS PERFECTLY BY ITSELF."

  - 10: "AI IS TRULY CREATIVE — IT INVENTS NEW IDEAS."
  - 11: "AI HAS ALREADY HELPED DISCOVER NEW MEDICINES."
  - 12: "AI CAN BE FOOLED BY THINGS A 5-YEAR-OLD WOULD GET RIGHT."

  - Answers: 6 ❌ · 7 ❌ · 8 ❌ · 9 ❌ · 10 ❌ · 11 ✅ · 12 ✅
  - Facilitator gives a 15-sec "reality" line after each (see speaker notes).
  - Wrong-as-a-room myths get a small 📎 *"Filed as Exhibit"* tag — seeds the courtroom callback.

### Slide 13 — Gate 1 Lit + "So What" (60 seconds)
- The KNOWLEDGE gate ignites amber on the strip (satisfying glow/sound cue).
- One line on screen: *You now know where AI is powerful — and where it breaks. That's pilot knowledge.*

**Facilitator 60-second delivery notes:**

1. **You didn't just memorize — you JUDGED.** "You just did something AI can't do for itself: you evaluated what's true and what's not about AI. The machine can't judge its own limits. You can."

2. **Both sides matter.** "Some of you lost points on the TRUEs — you underestimated what AI can do. Some lost points on the MYTHs — you overestimated it. A good pilot knows BOTH: what the machine can handle, and where it needs you to take over."

3. **This is the foundation for everything else today.** "For the rest of this session, every game builds on what you now know: AI is powerful, it's flawed, and the person steering it makes all the difference. That's you. Gate one — cleared."

*(Then move. Don't linger. The energy should carry forward, not deflate into a lecture.)*

## SECTION 2 — GATE 2: COMMUNICATION / Prompt Battle (Slides 14–22)

### Slide 14 — Gate Card
- 🎙️ **GATE 2 — COMMUNICATION** · *The AI is only as good as the human steering it.*
- Strip: KNOWLEDGE lit, COMMUNICATION pulsing.

### Slide 15 — "What's a Prompt?" (facilitator teaches, 90 seconds)
- Screen shows a simple visual: a stick figure → speech bubble → robot → output.
- One line below: **YOUR WORDS IN → AI'S WORK OUT**
- Below that, a box: **WHAT MAKES A GOOD PROMPT?**
  - 🎯 **WHO** — Who is the audience? (a 5-year-old? a teacher? a friend?)
  - 📐 **WHAT** — What format? (a list? a story? a rap? an email?)
  - 📏 **HOW MUCH** — How long? (3 lines? 1 paragraph? 30 seconds?)
  - 🎭 **WHAT TONE** — Fun? Serious? Sarcastic? Encouraging?
  - ⚡ **ONE EXTRA RULE** — Add one constraint that makes it interesting (e.g., "don't use the word 'good'")

**Facilitator notes (90 sec):**

"A prompt is just the instruction you give AI. Watch the difference:"

Show verbally (or point to screen):
- **Lazy prompt:** "Write about exams." → AI gives a boring paragraph nobody wants.
- **Pilot prompt:** "Write a 30-second pep talk for a nervous student before an exam. Make it sound like a coach in a locker room. Keep it under 5 sentences." → AI gives something you'd actually want to hear.

"Same AI. Same topic. The ONLY difference? Five extra seconds of thinking. That's what these 5 ingredients do — they turn a vague wish into a clear instruction. You're about to prove this."

*(Leave the 5 attributes visible on screen during the writing phase — it's their cheat-sheet.)*

### Slide 16 — Mission Briefing + Rules
- Screen: **PROMPT BATTLE — THE RULES**
- Three lines, big:
  - 1️⃣ TWO ROUNDS: one easy mission, one hard mission
  - 2️⃣ Write the BEST prompt you can for each (3 min per round)
  - 3️⃣ 1 random prompt goes LIVE + 1 gold standard — then you score the rest

**Facilitator notes for setup (2 min):**

1. Explain: "Round 1 is your warm-up — a simple mission. Round 2 is where real prompt skill shows. The 5 attributes on screen are your toolkit — use them."
2. All 12 squads collaborate — write ONE prompt per round on their **Prompt Bet Slip** (front = Round 1, back = Round 2).
3. Remind them: "The more ingredients from that list you use, the better your AI output will be. But don't just list them robotically — make it a clear instruction a human could follow too."

### Slide 17 — ROUND 1: EASY MISSION
- Screen top (stays small): The 5 prompt attributes as icons (WHO 🎯 WHAT 📐 HOW MUCH 📏 TONE 🎭 EXTRA ⚡)
- Center, big: **ROUND 1 — EASY MISSION:**
- **"Get AI to convince a teenager to drink more water."**
- Timer: **3:00**

**Why this mission works:**
- Universally relatable (everyone drinks water / doesn't drink enough).
- Many possible interpretations: funny ad? health facts? scare tactics? a meme caption? a rap? a text from a friend?
- Simple enough that even a lazy prompt will produce SOMETHING — but a great prompt will produce something clearly better.

### Slide 18 — ROUND 1: LIVE FIRE
- Screen: **LIVE FIRE — ROUND 1** · typing bay frame.

**Arena mechanic (same both rounds):**
1. Collect 12 slips (30 sec, captains pass forward).
2. **Random pull:** Shuffle face-down, pull 1 blindly. Announce squad name — instant drama.
3. **Type it EXACTLY as written** — no corrections, no edits, even if it has typos. Room watches output generate in real time.
4. Room scores: **1–5 fingers**. Helper notes average. That's the squad's score.
5. **Then type the Gold Standard:** "Now watch what happens when the prompt uses all 5 ingredients." Type the facilitator's pre-prepared prompt live. Room watches the output.
6. **Let the contrast land.** Pause 5 seconds. "Same AI. Same mission. Different pilot." No further lecture needed — the screen just proved it.

**Gold Standard prompt for Round 1 (type this second):**
> "Write a 4-line text message from a friend trying to guilt-trip a teenager into drinking water. Make it dramatic and funny, like they're dying of thirst on their behalf. Use gen-z slang. End with a water emoji."

### Slide 18b — Live: Random Squad's Prompt (dynamic)
- Typing bay + output. After: **SCORE IT! (1–5 fingers)**

### Slide 18c — Live: Gold Standard Prompt (dynamic)
- Typing bay + output. On screen after output: **SAME AI. SAME MISSION. DIFFERENT PILOT.**

### Slide 19 — ROUND 2: HARD MISSION
- Screen top: Same 5 prompt attributes (their cheat-sheet).
- Center, big: **ROUND 2 — HARD MISSION:**
- **"Get AI to help a student figure out what career might suit them — without asking the student any questions."**
- Timer: **3:00**

**Why this mission works:**
- Directly connects to the workshop's career-anxiety theme (plants the seed for Gate 4).
- Genuinely hard — how do you prompt AI to give career advice without a back-and-forth conversation? Forces creative prompt engineering.
- Many interpretations: a quiz it generates? a personality-based framework? a "if you like X try Y" list? a story that surfaces interests?
- The quality gap between lazy and sharp prompts will be HUGE here.

**Gold Standard prompt for Round 2 (type this second):**
> "Create a '5 Clues About Me' career discovery game for a teenager. Give 5 categories: what I do for fun, what frustrates me, what I'd fix in the world, what I lose track of time doing, what people ask me for help with. For each category, give 3 example answers and show what career family they might point to. Keep it casual and fun — not like a school test. Format as a simple table."

### Slide 20 — ROUND 2: LIVE FIRE
- Screen: **LIVE FIRE — ROUND 2** · typing bay frame.

### Slide 20b — Live: Random Squad's Prompt (dynamic)
- Typing bay + output. After: **SCORE IT! (1–5 fingers)**

### Slide 20c — Live: Gold Standard Prompt (dynamic)
- Typing bay + output. On screen: **SAME AI. SAME MISSION. DIFFERENT PILOT.**

### Slide 21 — Peer Scoring (both rounds)
- Screen: **YOUR TURN TO JUDGE** · *Read the prompt you received. Score 1–5. The gold standard is your benchmark.*
- Timer: 3:00

**Facilitator notes — peer scoring:**
1. Redistribute remaining 11 slips per round (pass one table to the left — ensuring no squad scores their own).
2. Each squad READS the received prompt aloud within their group.
3. Scores 1–5: How close is this to the gold standard? Did it use the 5 attributes?
4. Write score on slip, pass back. Helper collects.
5. Final squad score for Gate 2 = average of their two rounds (live finger-vote for the 2 random squads, peer score for the other 10).

### Slide 22 — Gate 2 Lit + "So What" (60 seconds)
- COMMUNICATION gate ignites amber.
- One line on screen: *Same mission. Different steering. The skill is yours.*

**Facilitator 60-second delivery notes:**

Cover TWO beats (30 sec each):

1. **Round 1 vs Round 2.** "Notice something? In Round 1, most prompts did okay — the mission was simple. In Round 2, the gap between great prompts and lazy prompts got HUGE. That's the lesson: the harder the problem, the more your communication skill matters. AI is powerful — but it's only as smart as the instruction you give it."

2. **This is the co-pilot skill.** "You just practiced the most important AI skill that exists: clear thinking turned into clear communication. The students who get good at this — who learn to steer precisely — will outperform people who just type 'help me with my career' and hope for the best. Gate two — cleared."

*(Move. Energy is high — carry it into the court.)*

## SECTION 3 — GATE 3: ETHICS / AI Ethics Court (Slides 23–29)

### Slide 23 — Gate Card
- ⚖️ **GATE 3 — ETHICS** · *The machine is never guilty. The pilot always is.*
- Strip: KNOWLEDGE + COMMUNICATION lit, ETHICS pulsing.

### Slide 23b — "Why Ethics?" (facilitator teaches, 90 seconds)
- Screen shows a simple visual: a **balance scale** — one side has a gavel, the other has a laptop.
- Below: **POWER WITHOUT RULES = DANGER**
- Under that, 4 lines:
  - 🤝 **INTEGRITY** — Be honest about what's yours and what's AI's
  - 🔍 **DILIGENCE** — Check the work. Verify. Don't trust blindly.
  - 🛡️ **DUTY TO OTHERS** — Don't use AI to harm, trick, or expose others
  - 📣 **TRANSPARENCY** — If AI helped, say so. No hiding.

**Facilitator notes (90 sec):**

"Before we open our court, let me ask you something. Every serious profession in the world has an ethics code — rules about what you WON'T do, even when you CAN.

Doctors CAN prescribe any drug — but their code says: first, do no harm. They won't give you medicine you don't need just because they could.

Lawyers CAN know a client's darkest secrets — but their code says: protect that trust. They won't share it, even if it benefits them.

Engineers CAN cut corners on a building to save money — but their code says: public safety first. They won't risk lives for a shortcut.

Financial professionals CAN trade on insider information — but their code says: be fair, be transparent, earn trust honestly.

Every one of these codes comes down to the same idea: just because you CAN do something doesn't mean you SHOULD. Power without rules is dangerous. And the people who last in any career — the ones people trust, promote, and respect — are the ones who chose ethics over shortcuts.

Now — AI has just handed YOU more power than any generation of students has ever had. You can generate essays, fake images, copy someone's style, expose private information — in seconds. So the question is: what's YOUR code? What will you refuse to do, even when it's easy?

That's what we're about to find out. Three real people used AI. Three complaints have been filed. You decide: guilty or not guilty."

### Slide 24 — Court Setup
- Screen: **AI ETHICS COURT** · *3 cases. 3 defendants. You're the justice system.*
- Below: a courtroom layout showing PROSECUTION (left), DEFENSE (right), JURY (center). Each labeled with squad numbers for Case 1.
- Sub-line: *The AI is not on trial. The HUMAN who used it is.*

**Facilitator notes — setting up the court (30 sec):**

"Here's how it works. I'm going to read you three real-world cases — adults who used AI and got into legal trouble. For each case, I'll assign squads. Some of you argue the person is GUILTY. Some argue NOT GUILTY. The rest are the JURY — you vote.

Remember: the AI didn't choose anything. It did what it was told. The question is always — did the HUMAN cross the line?"

Announce Case 1 rotation: "Squads 1 and 2 — you're prosecution. Squads 3 and 4 — defence. Everyone else — you're the jury."

**Squad rotation:**

| Case | Prosecution | Defense | Jury |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Squads 1 + 2 | Squads 3 + 4 | Squads 5–12 |
| 2 | Squads 5 + 6 | Squads 7 + 8 | Squads 1–4, 9–12 |
| 3 | Squads 9 + 10 | Squads 11 + 12 | Squads 1–8 |

Every squad argues once. Every squad is jury twice.

**Running each case (3 min per case):**
1. Show the FIR slide. Read it aloud dramatically (like a police report).
2. Prosecution squads huddle 30 sec → ONE speaker argues 45 sec.
3. Defense squads huddle 30 sec → ONE speaker argues 45 sec.
4. Jury (8 squads): hold up GUILTY / NOT-GUILTY cards. Count majority.
5. Facilitator drops the verdict + the rule (30 sec).

### Slide 25 — CASE 1: "THE CODE LEAK"

```
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
⚖️ CASE NO. 001
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

COMPLAINANT: TechNova Solutions Pvt Ltd, Bengaluru
DEFENDANT:   Rahul Mehta, Software Developer

CHARGE:      Leaking confidential client data

WHAT HAPPENED:
Rahul was debugging code at midnight before a
deadline. He pasted proprietary company code into
ChatGPT to find the bug faster. The code contained
a client's database login credentials. The company's
security team flagged the breach. The client is now
threatening legal action.

RAHUL SAYS:  "I was just trying to fix a bug. I
didn't even notice the credentials were in there.
Everyone uses AI to code faster."
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

- Corner: **GUILTY? or NOT GUILTY?**

**Facilitator notes — talking points each side might raise:**

| Prosecution (GUILTY) | Defense (NOT GUILTY) |
|---|---|
| He signed an NDA — he's responsible for what leaves the system, period. | He didn't intend to leak anything — it was an honest mistake under pressure. |
| "I didn't notice" isn't a legal defense — negligence IS a breach. | The company should have tools to prevent sensitive data from being pasted. It's a systems failure, not a personal one. |
| The client's data is now in a third-party AI system FOREVER — the harm is real and irreversible. | Every developer uses AI to debug. The company benefits from faster output — they should have trained staff on what's safe to share. |
| If a doctor accidentally gave the wrong medicine, "I didn't notice" wouldn't save them. Same standard. | He was working at midnight to MEET THEIR DEADLINE. The pressure came from the company itself. |

**How to evaluate the jury vote:**
- This case tests: **Does intent matter when real harm occurs?** The mature answer: intent reduces moral blame but doesn't erase legal responsibility. You're still accountable for what you feed AI, even accidentally.
- Reasonable verdict: **GUILTY of negligence** (not malice). He should have checked. The company ALSO failed (no guardrails), but that doesn't clear him.

**Rule revealed:** **PROTECT** — be careful what data you feed AI. Once it's in, you can't take it back. Always check before you paste.

```
⚖️ CASE NO. 002
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

COMPLAINANT: Family of late singer S.P. Balasubrahmanyam
DEFENDANT:   Deepak Sharma, YouTube Creator

CHARGE:      Voice cloning & impersonation for profit

WHAT HAPPENED:
Deepak used an AI tool to clone the voice of
legendary singer S.P. Balasubrahmanyam — who
passed away in 2020 — and released 5 "new songs"
on YouTube. He earned ₹2 lakh in ad revenue. He
never disclosed the voice was AI-generated. Fans
believed they were unreleased tracks and shared
them emotionally.

DEEPAK SAYS: "I'm honouring his legacy. The songs
are my original compositions — only the voice is
AI. I'm keeping his music alive for new fans."
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

- Corner: **GUILTY? or NOT GUILTY?**

**Facilitator notes — talking points each side might raise:**

| Prosecution (GUILTY) | Defense (NOT GUILTY) |
|---|---|
| SPB is dead — he cannot consent. Using a dead person's voice without family permission is identity theft. | He's a fan paying tribute. Musicians cover songs all the time — how is this different? |
| Fans were DECEIVED — they cried, shared emotional messages, believing these were real unreleased tracks. That's manipulation. | He never explicitly claimed they were "real SPB songs." People assumed. That's their mistake. |
| He earned ₹2 lakh. This isn't tribute — it's profit from someone else's identity. | The compositions are 100% original. Only the voice timbre was cloned. The creative work is his. |
| The family never approved this. Their grief is being used as content. | SPB would have wanted his voice to live on. Art belongs to the public after an artist passes. |
| If someone deepfaked YOUR parent's voice to sell things, would that be okay? | Copyright law doesn't cover a "voice." There's no clear legal violation yet. |

**How to evaluate the jury vote:**
- This case tests: **Can you "honour" someone while profiting from their identity without consent?** The emotional weight is heavy — SPB is beloved and gone. The mature answer: tribute requires transparency and family consent. Profiting without disclosure crosses from homage to exploitation.
- Reasonable verdict: **GUILTY of impersonation for profit.** The non-disclosure is the killer — had he labeled every video "AI-generated tribute, not real SPB," this would be a different conversation.

**Rule revealed:** **CREDIT** — be transparent. If AI created it, say so. If it's someone else's voice/identity, you don't own it just because you pressed a button.

| Prosecution (GUILTY) | Defense (NOT GUILTY) |
|---|---|
| They NAMED her in the prompt. They deliberately targeted her specific style. That's not inspiration — that's theft with extra steps. | Copyright law protects specific artworks, not a "style." Every artist is influenced by others. That's how art works. |

- Reasonable verdict: **GUILTY of unfair practice.** The deliberate naming in the prompt converts "inspiration" into "targeting." Even if the law hasn't caught up, the ethics are clear.

**Rule revealed:** **THINK FIRST** — just because AI makes it easy doesn't make it right. Ask yourself: would I do this to their face?

**General evaluation guidance for the facilitator:**

When dropping the verdict after jury votes:
1. **Acknowledge the grey.** "This is a genuinely hard case. There's no perfect answer — but here's how professionals would see it..."
2. **Don't shame the "wrong" voters.** "If you voted differently — good. That means you were thinking. The point isn't to agree with me. The point is to THINK before you act."
3. **Connect to the students' future.** "When YOU use AI in your career — coding, creating, working — you'll face versions of this case. The code helps you decide in advance."

## SECTION 4 — GATE 4: NAVIGATION / Where Do You Fly (Slides 29–34) — THE HEART

### Slide 29 — Gate Card
- 🧭 **GATE 4 — NAVIGATION** · *Where do YOU want to fly?*

### Slides 30–31 — Today / Future Jobs
- ONE surprising job title per beat, big, with **TODAY? or FUTURE?** prompt.
- Quick-fire: show title → squads vote by card → reveal → facilitator gives one-line context → next.
- 8 rounds, ~45 sec each:
  - "PROMPT ENGINEER" → 🟢 TODAY (Pays ₹20–50 LPA right now. Didn't exist 3 years ago.)
  - "AI BIAS AUDITOR" → 🟢 TODAY (Companies hire people to find discrimination in AI systems.)
  - "DRONE TRAFFIC CONTROLLER" → 🟢 TODAY (Aviation authorities already employ them.)
  - "DIGITAL TWIN ARCHITECT" → 🟢 TODAY (Builds virtual replicas of factories and cities for simulation.)
  - "AI-HUMAN COLLABORATION COACH" → 🔮 FUTURE (Will train teams to work alongside AI — like a fitness coach for human+AI productivity.)
